# V7-Turkish-coffee — The Heritage Coffee Proof Venture

A founder playbook for turning an eight-year, hand-perfected Turkish coffee method into a
**premium heritage coffee brand**, and then using that brand's proof to revive the heritage
**sherbet line at V7 / V-Cola** (see the companion repo `vcola-tech-strategy`, Docs 12–15).

> **Status:** Draft v1 (November 2026). Founder working document, not investment advice.
> Market facts are cited in [`docs/references.md`](docs/references.md); prices and scenarios
> marked *estimate* or *hypothesis* are mine and must be validated with paid pilots (Doc 07).

---

## The idea in one paragraph

Omar spent about a year researching historic Turkish coffee preparation, including interviews
with Cairo's oldest coffeehouses, then about seven years of trial and error. The result is a
specific protocol: high-altitude **Yemeni Arabica**, light roast, **9:1 coffee to cardamom**,
optional **Chios mastic** or **Persian saffron**, a side of lightly roasted **pistachios**, and a
slow, cold-start, hand-stirred brew in a copper cezve. The decision this repo makes is to
**preserve the process by hand** rather than build a machine now, and to sell it as a
craft-and-provenance experience before scaling anything.

## The verdict up front

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| Can it compete with *all* coffee? | **No, and it shouldn't try.** It can't beat espresso chains on convenience or supermarket Turkish coffee on price. |
| Where can it win? | The **top of the Turkish/Arabic coffee pyramid**: a provenance-rich, ceremonial cup that sits between a good café double and a Wild Fox–style $100 tasting. |
| How strong is the position? | Strong in **taste + ritual + story**, medium in **sourcing defensibility**, weak in **throughput and scalability** while it stays manual. |
| Realistic size by hand | One flagship counter is roughly a **$100–250k/year** business (estimate, Doc 05). Small, but enough to prove the brand. |
| The scalable part | The **packaged home blend** and the **brand/provenance system**, which transfers directly to V7's sherbet line. |
| Biggest risks | Yemen supply (war, sanctions, transport), labour-limited throughput, and overclaiming health or "Sultan's recipe" stories. |

Full reasoning: [Doc 04 — Competitive landscape](docs/04-competitive-landscape.md).

## Sequencing (the short version)

1. **Now (month 0–1):** write the spec (Doc 01), run the blind tests (Doc 07, Gate 1).
2. **Month 1–3:** home blend in small batches + a weekend tasting counter in Cairo; paid price tests.
3. **Month 3–9:** if repeat purchase holds, a permanent counter or residency in a premium venue;
   train the second and third brewers (Doc 06).
4. **Month 9+:** Gulf pop-up and export of the home blend; bring the evidence to V7 (Doc 10).
