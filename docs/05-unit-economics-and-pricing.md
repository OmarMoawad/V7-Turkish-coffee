# 05 — Unit Economics & Pricing

All figures are **estimates** built from the sourced inputs below, at **EGP 56.3 per USD**
(late October 2026). Treat prices as hypotheses to test with paid pilots (Doc 07). The
calculation script is reproducible: every number here comes from the assumptions table.

## 1. Inputs

| Input | Value used | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Yemeni green coffee | $75–95/kg landed in Egypt | TYPICA lists Yemeni lots at $65–68/kg FOB; + freight, duty, handling (estimate) |
| Roast weight loss | 16% | Typical for light roasts (estimate) |
| Roasted cost | **$89–113/kg ≈ EGP 5,000–6,400/kg** | Derived |
| Cardamom (top grade) | $35/kg | Estimate; small share of cost |
| Saffron (Super Negin) | $1.88/g wholesale, up to 3× retail | Iranian wholesale ~$1,881/kg (Jan 2025) |
| Chios mastic | $150–300/kg | Estimate (retail range) |
| Pistachios | $30–50/kg | Estimate |
| Labour | EGP 100 per brewer-hour, fully loaded | **Assumption**; replace with real wage data |
| Throughput | 5 pots per brewer-hour | ~10–12 min per pot incl. ~1,000 stirs |

One **pot** = 200–250 ml, which serves **two generous cups** (or one large ceremonial serving).

## 2. Cost per pot

| Pot | Ingredients | + labour (EGP 20) + consumables & waste (EGP 15–20) |
|---|---|---|
| Signature (18–22 g coffee + cardamom + sugar) | **EGP ~96–146** ($1.70–2.59) | **EGP ~130–181** |
| Reserve (+ mastic *or* saffron, + pistachio side) | **EGP ~135–222** ($2.41–3.94) | **EGP ~175–262** |

For comparison, **the coffee alone in a Signature pot costs more than Caribou charges for a
Turkish double (EGP 78.75)**. This product can't be priced near chain cafés.

Compare the bean cost per 20 g: supermarket Turkish coffee is about **EGP 15 (Abu Auf)** to
**EGP 40 (Al Ameed)**; this coffee is about **EGP 100–128**. So the founder's "8–10×" instinct is
roughly right against mass brands, and closer to 3× against the premium supermarket tier.

## 3. Price ladder — Cairo (hypotheses to test)

| Tier | What's included | Test price | Contribution margin* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Signature pot** (for two) | Yemeni + cardamom, sugar level of choice, water, a story card | **EGP 400–450** (350 as the low test) | ~48% at 350 · ~60% at 450 |
| **Reserve pot** (for two) | + Chios mastic *or* Persian saffron, pistachio dish | **EGP 650–900** | ~60% at 650 · ~71% at 900 |
| **Ceremony** (2–4 people, ~45 min) | Three pots (Signature, Mastic, Saffron), brewed live, history told, pistachios and sweets | **EGP 2,500–4,000** per table | High; sells time and story |
| **Home blend, 250 g** | Roasted to order, with brewing card; mastic/saffron sachets as add-ons | **EGP 2,000–2,800** | Bag cost EGP ~1,250–1,550 → ~23–55% (aim for EGP 2,400+) |

\*Contribution margin = price minus ingredients, brewer labour and consumables; **before rent,
management and marketing.** At EGP 350 the Signature is thin once rent is included. **Use
EGP 400–450 as the baseline.**

Per person, a Signature pot at EGP 400–450 is about EGP 200–225, roughly **2.5–3× a Caribou
double**. That's a premium price, but one an upscale Cairo customer can choose for an occasion.

## 5. What one hand-brewed counter can earn (scenarios)

| Scenario | Pots/day | Avg price | Revenue/year | ≈ USD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekend-heavy counter | 40 | EGP 400 | EGP 5.8M | $102k |
| Busy flagship | 60 | EGP 400 | EGP 8.6M | $153k |
| Flagship with strong Reserve/Ceremony mix | 60 | EGP 650 | EGP 14.0M | $249k |

Sixty pots a day needs about **12 brewer-hours**, i.e. two trained brewers on a full day. That
is the binding constraint: **revenue scales with trained brewers, not with tables.**

## 6. What this means

- **Manual = small and premium.** One counter is a ~$100–250k/year business at best. That's
  enough to prove the brand and fund training, but not a large company by itself.
- **The home blend is the scalable product.** It needs roasting capacity and packaging, not
  brewer-hours. Most long-term revenue likely comes from here.
- **The stir test matters financially** (Doc 03 §5). If 300 stirs match 1,000 blind, throughput
  rises and labour cost per pot falls substantially.
- **Price above, not near, the chains.** Competing at EGP 100–150 would lose money on every pot.
