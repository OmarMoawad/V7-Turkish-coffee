# 06 — Process Preservation (No Machine)

The decision: **keep the method manual** and protect eight years of research and trials. The
asset is know-how that currently lives in one person. If that person is unavailable, the business
stops. This doc turns tacit skill into a system that survives, can be taught, and is hard to copy.

## 1. Codify: the Master Protocol

- **Written spec** (Doc 01), versioned, with measured parameters rather than feel words.
- **Video protocol:** film the founder brewing 20+ pots from several angles, with a thermometer
  and timer in frame. Annotate each stage: cold start, first foam, stir rhythm, pour point.
- **Stove calibration card:** for any new burner, measure time to heat 200 ml of 4 °C water to
  90 °C; adjust flame until it matches the founder's reference. This makes "lowest flame"
  portable to new locations.
- **Sensory reference:** freeze-dried or vacuum-sealed reference samples of the roast, plus a
  short flavour vocabulary for the house cup (cardamom intensity, body, sweetness, foam).
- **Test log archive:** every experiment from Doc 03, kept permanently. It's the evidence of the
  eight years and the base for future improvements.

## 2. Teach: the brewer apprenticeship

A guild model (usta → kalfa → çırak in Ottoman crafts) suits the brand and the reality.

| Level | Requirement to pass |
|---|---|
| Apprentice | Brews 100 logged pots; passes a blind triangle test where tasters can't tell their pot from the founder's in at least 2 of 3 rounds |
| Brewer | Passes monthly blind checks for 3 months; can calibrate a new stove |
| Master | Can train apprentices; signs off on quality at a location |

Budget roughly **6–10 weeks per apprentice** before solo service (estimate). Train the second
brewer before opening permanently, and the third before any second site.

## 3. Protect: making it hard to copy

Recipes are generally **not patentable**, and copyright doesn't protect a method. Protection is
layered instead:

| Layer | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Trade secret** | Keep exact roast profile, dose ratios, rest window and supplier details confidential; written NDAs with every staff member and roasting partner; need-to-know access |
| **Trademark** | Register the brand name and logo in Egypt early, then in the Gulf and Türkiye before export |
| **Supplier relationships** | Long-term, named relationships with Yemeni producers or importers; hard to replicate quickly |
| **People** | Fair pay and status for trained brewers; master brewers are the real moat |
| **Brand and story** | First credible "best Turkish coffee in Cairo" with documented provenance. Hard to displace once earned |
| **Proof** | Blind-test results and paid-pilot data, published selectively, build credibility competitors lack |

## 4. Quality control at service

- Daily: one pot per brewer tasted blind against the reference by the lead.
- Weekly: grinder calibration, stove calibration check, roast batch tasting.
- Each batch: roast date, origin lot, cupping notes logged.
- Customer signals: track repeat visits and sugar-level choices.

## 5. When to revisit a machine

Revisit only when **all three** are true: demand exceeds what trained brewers can serve; blind
tests show which variables really matter (Doc 03); and the home blend is profitable. At that
point the realistic route is **adapting an existing commercial Turkish coffee machine** to the
measured heat curve, not inventing one. Automatic Turkish coffee machines already exist for home
and commercial use, so the value lies in the protocol, not the hardware.
