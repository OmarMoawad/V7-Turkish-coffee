# 14 — Tier Architecture: Five Products, Two Brands

The plan has grown from one cup into a portfolio. This doc fixes what each tier is, who makes
it (human or machine), and which brand it wears.

## 1. The tiers

| Tier | What it is | Made by | Brand | Price signal (Cairo, hypothesis) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ceremony** | Live table service: three pots, history told, pistachios and sweets | **Master brewer, by hand** | Master brand | EGP 2,500–4,000 per table |
| **Executive line** ("your kahvecibaşı") | A master brews at the client's home, office or majlis, or a retained morning service | **Master brewer, by hand** | Master brand | Retainer or per-visit; highest margin per labour-hour |
| **Reserve** | Mastic or saffron pot, pistachio dish | Machine, with a human host | Master brand | EGP 650–900 per pot |
| **Signature** | Yemeni + cardamom pot | Machine, with a human host | Master brand | EGP 400–450 per pot (≈ EGP 200–225 per cup) |
| **Daily** | Honest single cup, lab-certified 100% coffee, same cardamom profile | Machine | **Separate brand** | ~EGP 50 per cup |
| **Home packs** | Pre-dosed packs and 100 g / 200 g / 500 g / gift bags | Facility | Both, separately | See Doc 05 |

## 2. Why the Ceremony stays human

At EGP 2,500+ per table the customer buys theatre and closeness to the craft. A machine brewing
silently at that price removes the story. So the Ceremony and Executive line stay hand-brewed by
masters, and they are also where new masters are trained (Doc 06). The machine is presented in
the other tiers as "the precision instrument of the house protocol", which turns it into a
selling point. If the founder later wants a machine-led Ceremony, test it with paying customers
first.

The Ottoman palace had a **kahvecibaşı**, the chief coffee maker. The Executive line borrows that
idea honestly: a personal master brewer, not a claim about any Sultan's recipe (Doc 02).

## 3. The Executive line

- Customer: senior professionals, hosts, boardrooms, hotel suites, Ramadan and corporate gifting.
- The morning-commute version depends on transit preservation (Doc 12 §6). Until that's solved,
  offer brew-on-site only.
- Low capex, no machine dependency, cash from early on; it can start before the machine exists.

## 4. The Daily tier: separate brand, honest proposition

- **Proposition:** the everyday Turkish coffee most people drink, made honestly: 100% coffee,
  lab-certified, published certificates, consistent machine brewing, about EGP 50.
- **Where it sits:** around today's café Turkish coffee (Caribou lists EGP 63.75 single), above a
  home cup of supermarket coffee (about EGP 15–40 of coffee per cup).
- **Bean:** a commercial Arabica blend or **fine Robusta**, which is a recognised graded
  category. Robusta handled badly turns harsh, so it must pass the same blind rigour, tested
  against premium supermarket coffee brewed well.
- **Margin (estimate):** cost about EGP 12–13 per cup → roughly 74–77% contribution at EGP 50.
- **Claim discipline:** say "100% coffee, certified" with proof. Don't accuse competitors of
  cheating.
- **Why a separate brand:** if the EGP 50 cup wears the same face as the EGP 450 Yemeni pot, the
  premium halo dilutes. Same honesty doctrine and quality system underneath, different name and
  shelf identity (the house-of-brands approach in `vcola-tech-strategy` Doc 19).
- **Role in the franchise:** daily habit traffic for operators; customers can trade up to the
  Signature.

## 5. Menu discipline at launch

Launch with **two products** per format: Signature plus one Reserve. Add more only once demand is
proven. Don't build every tier at once (sequencing in the README).
