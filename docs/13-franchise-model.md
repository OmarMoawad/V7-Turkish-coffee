# 13 — The Franchise Model: Machine + Consumable + Brand Standards

The founder's plan: give an aspiring operator **the machine and the ingredients**, let the
operator's storefront advertise the brand, and keep prices disciplined. This turns the coffee
from a labour-bound craft into a platform. It also changes where defensibility comes from:
v1 relied on trained brewers (Doc 06); the franchise adds a **hardware-and-supply moat**.

## 1. What the operator gets

- A calibrated machine (Doc 12), leased or sold, with maintenance.
- Sealed, pre-dosed packs for each product on the menu, supplied only by the brand.
- Brand identity, menu, serving ritual, training (days, not years), and launch marketing.
- The right to a defined territory.

## 2. What the brand gets

- Recurring revenue from packs (the main profit line) and machine lease or sale.
- Every storefront as advertising, plus a co-op marketing fund.
- Sales data from every machine (cups by product, time of day), which feeds the forecasting
  approach in `vcola-tech-strategy` Doc 02.

## 3. Price discipline without illegal price-fixing

Dictating an independent operator's retail price is **resale price maintenance**, which is
restricted under competition law in many jurisdictions; Egypt's competition law, and regimes in
the Gulf and Türkiye, need checking. Don't promise operators "enforced prices." Get the same
result commercially:

- **Recommended retail prices** printed on brand menus.
- **Brand standards** in the licence: product spec, serving ritual, cleanliness, presentation.
- **Supply dependency**: operators keep their machine and pack supply by meeting standards.
- **Co-op advertising** and promotions available to operators who follow the brand's pricing
  guidance.

Have an Egyptian competition lawyer review the licence before the first operator signs.

## 4. Operator selection

Vet operators rather than selling to anyone: location quality, capital, a site visit, and a
signed brand-standards agreement. Start with **2–3 operators in one city** and instrument them
the same way as the flagship (Doc 07).

## 5. Quality control across operators

- Machines log every brew cycle; deviations flag remotely.
- Mystery-shopper blind checks monthly against the reference pot.
- Pack lot numbers tie every cup back to a roast batch.

## 6. Target customer: own a defined occasion, not all coffee

The ambition is that anyone seeking good Turkish coffee finds it pointless to look elsewhere.
That's achievable **within defined occasions**: the premium café cup, hosting guests, gifting,
the executive morning. The home brewer drinking supermarket coffee isn't the Signature customer.
The separate daily tier (Doc 14) is the brand's answer for that person, under its own name.

## 7. Gates before franchising

1. Machine parity passed (Doc 07, Gate M).
2. Flagship profitable for six months with repeat-purchase data.
3. Pack shelf-life validated.
4. Licence reviewed by counsel; trademarks registered.
